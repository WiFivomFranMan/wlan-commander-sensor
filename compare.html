A WLANPi Go and a NanoPi Zero2 with Osprey antennas, swept minutes apart. The Go carries two radios; only its Intel BE200 is compared here, against the NanoPi’s. Same silicon, same driver, same app — so what is left is antennas, enclosure, and one hand.

Read this before the numbers

what this run can and cannot say
The Go was handicapped, deliberately

The Go was held in a hand, MagSafe-attached to a phone, which covers most of its antennas. This run therefore measures a held Go against a free-standing NanoPi, not two antenna designs on equal terms. A second run with both devices face up and untouched is the one that isolates the antennas.

Only the Go's BE200 is compared

The Go ran two different radios and split the channel plan between them with zero overlap: its BE200 (wlanpi1) took 31 frequencies including all the 6 GHz, and its built-in dual-band Broadcom (wlanpi0) took the other 17 and never went above 5825 MHz.

Pooling the two would mean comparing the NanoPi's BE200 against a Broadcom on a third of the channels. Doing exactly that turned a 2 dB Go advantage on 2.4 GHz into an apparent 5.6 dB NanoPi advantage — an artefact of which radio happened to own the channel. Only wlanpi1 is used below.

Coverage is a device question, not an antenna one

Across both radios the Go visited 48 frequencies to the NanoPi's 49, so at device level the sweeps are equivalent. But the Go's BE200 alone saw only 31 of them, which is why the “heard by only one” counts below favour the NanoPi so heavily. That is the Go parallelising its sweep, not the NanoPi hearing better.

How the numbers were made

method

Beacons only, p95 of RSSI in dBm. Access points are paired by BSSID and frequency, never by name, because multi-BSSID radios reuse one SSID across bands.

Each BSSID is assigned a home frequency — the modal one, tie-broken by median RSSI — and beacons heard while the radio was tuned elsewhere are discarded. Those arrive attenuated through an adjacent channel and would drag the distribution down unevenly.

A pair needs at least 5 beacons on both sides to appear; 6 were dropped for being thinner than that. Hidden and malformed SSIDs are excluded, as are any that fail to decode as printable UTF-8.

Both captures came from WLAN Commander 1.1.0 on iOS, 54 channels at 200 ms dwell, 20 MHz wide. At that dwell an access point yields roughly a dozen beacons per sweep, which is enough for a p95 and not enough to call a 1 dB difference real.

The Go's radios were identified from the captures rather than from metadata, which names only one interface: wlanpi1 reaches 7095 MHz and captured 2,689 frames of 6 GHz, wlanpi0 stops at 5825 MHz and captured none. Their frequency sets do not intersect at all.
